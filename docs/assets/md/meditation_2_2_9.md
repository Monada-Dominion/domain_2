# 2.2.9 Multidimensional Meditation – Theory – The Triangle (Angle of Displacement)

## Triangle and Rhombus (figures 3–4)

This block is dedicated to figures numbered 3–4, that is, the triangle and the rhombus. In the previous blocks, we went over different techniques of concentration on the point, the line, and the state that these figures convey, their mutual connection, and their independent meanings. Today we will focus on the triangle, and specifically on the triangle as the angle of a line’s displacement.

As you may remember, the figures develop one from another. The point begins with awareness of it as emptiness, as infinite division and falling, then as an independent unit or the possibility for something to exist. Its connection with the line is formed as a result of a change in one’s relation to this point, or as a result of its infinite falling or filling. Also, there is movement in the line.

And the line, as an independent element, gives us this state of understanding that there is some movement in two directions: up and down, left and right at the same time.

All of this is directly connected with abstract ideas, which is what turns these simple ideas about concentration into a truly deep meditation, if you perform them sequentially or concentrate on one of the stages for a long time.

As I have already said, the ultimate goal of meditation is the skill of evoking, or the ability to evoke, one state or another through a certain concentration on one figure or another.

Roughly speaking, the transition to each next figure, to each next stage, already implies the ability to hold concentration on the previous stages. To understand the line, you need to understand the point: try to concentrate on the point and learn to immerse yourself in a certain state in order to deepen it, strengthen it. If you now, before you begin to perceive the next amount of information, try to concentrate first on the point: the point as emptiness, the point as a unit; on the line; on movement along the line.

---

The triangle, as the angle of a line’s displacement, is formed as a result of shifting the line. Obviously.

If we look at the line in this way, then at one of its points it remains motionless, and the other point shifts. This is how an angle is formed. And what does this tell us?

First of all, the third point appears as a factor influencing probability or movement. We need to recall that the first point existed by itself as the possible existence of an event, and the second point, at least as the direction of its movement, became the probability of realizing this possibility in a certain quality. And the third point is a factor influencing the probability of realizing the possibility, or determining how this entire possibility will be realized.

To begin the meditation, relax, breathe. Focus on the point, then on the line; after that, mentally imagine three coordinate axes, or three directions.

Also the direction toward yourself and away from yourself.

The last stage of the previous block implied beginning movement in two directions.

Choose one of these movements for yourself and begin this movement, but now focus on the idea that even within one movement — for example, upward—there can be different qualities, and this can be movement within an axis or within some kind of flow. There can be movement outside this axis, and there can be boundary movement. What I am talking about.

The line that is formed as a result of movement—and this movement between states, movement in immersion into different meditative states, movement in life. This can be development or relocation in the social environment, some spiritual rise or descent—all of this is the idea of movement.

It can be within, or not within, this system that forms these possibilities at all.

So what is this system?

For a human being, the intersection of three axes—abstract three axes—is the connection of their threefold nature.

This is their physical body, their social life, and their spiritual component.

Accordingly, what you choose for yourself—for example, movement along the horizontal—is a reflection of social movement and immersion into the idea of social development.

Movement downward or inward is physical development, self-development.

And movement upward is spiritual development. The transformation of your state.

And if we take movement upward—immersion into reflections on the nature of our soul, on the existence of life on the planet at all, on the meaning of this life — then we can have different qualities of this immersion.

And the closer it is, the more this line of movement is displaced in the horizontal plane, the more apparent the mixture, or the ratio, of this spiritual and the social becomes.

Likewise, there can be social and physical, and different qualities.

How is this to be understood?

In each of these relations, one possibility or another is realized.

In terms of the physical body, the possibility of the life and development of our physical body is realized. In terms of social development, there is the realization of some aspect of our personality and our life, interaction with other people.

In spiritual terms, there is the possibility of the development of our soul and various spiritual qualities.

The probability that it is precisely this possibility that is realized is determined by how and where we move.

Conditionally, if our movement goes upward and our first point is at the origin, then this is, as it were, the embodiment of the idea of ascent.

If at the center of the coordinates the movement goes downward, then this is, on the contrary, a descent from the spiritual, or from the not very spiritual, to the minimally spiritual — to immersion in the physical, fleshly state.

The last stage of the previous block implied movement in two directions. Why is that? Because in any case there is constantly some rise, an ascending stream. And this can be compared to transferring the result from the material plane into the spiritual, non-material one: the production of some meanings, some energy and information. And likewise, from there information goes down as well and becomes denser.

All together, this exists simultaneously, and therefore it is precisely two-way movement that we needed to become aware of.

But even this two-way movement can shift, and then movement in two directions becomes, to a greater or lesser degree, directed into another axis.

As I said, if we chose the spiritual one and begin to turn it like this up to 90 degrees, we begin to connect with the social sphere.

And this immersion into meditation, these abstract figures, here can already acquire a certain practical connection with the real world, when you overlay onto these meanings the meanings from your real life.

What interests you in terms of spiritual self-development? And this movement upward — how do you see ascent in terms of spiritual self-realization, what do you understand by it; what do you see as physical development, what as social.

The social can also be directed toward serving the external world and other people, or toward developing certain relationships with people to your own benefit, that is, gaining benefit from the external world.

And the angle of displacement of this line, which forms the triangle, gives us, as a result, two lines connected at one point.

They are connected, of course, at the point of the realization of the possibility.

Either it exists, or it does not exist. This is the first point, which is infinitely divided and can be filled with its presence, with some kind of awareness. It remains motionless. If it is not there, if it shifts, then everything changes differently.

And the other line is the connection with the probability that the possibility is realized in this way.

At the same moment, it is the connection with the factor that influences probability and the realization of possibilities through probability. That is, between them yet another line is formed here, which gives the triangle the possibility to exist.

This is the line of connection of the probability of realizing the possibility—that is, the actual choice of this movement and the existence of the line as such, consisting of many points, of moving between these states.

And the connection with the factor influencing this probability and the possibility separately. This factor is precisely the choice of the direction of movement and the choice of where and how this movement will be directed.

That is, if our point is simply a beginning, we choose, for example, movement upward, allow movement upward and downward at the same time—it can be pure spirituality.

It can be located somewhere within what limits this pure movement.

With a small displacement, it can be located on the boundary of that flow which determines pure spirituality.

That is, the non-mixing of the spiritual and the material can be characterized as pure spirituality. It can be somewhere on the boundary, as a light touch or awareness of the connection between these two worlds, the material and the non-material.

And it can be transferred into a purely practical plane, that is, a material one, when we do not touch what we can only feel, but consider this spiritual ascent directly as a connection, probably, of the laws of the world above and the world below—that is, the material and the non-material world.*

We already consider rather the material world and its spiritual nature, but we do not immerse into it.

And this is, accordingly, such a light movement upward, but not along the axis of spirituality, but somewhere between spirituality and the social sphere—that is, at an angle of 45 degrees.

We depart from the point, but already form a kind of triangle.

That is, we have the possibility of this spiritual development, we have the probability that we made this choice, and the factor of how this happens—that our movement goes not quite upward, but it goes sideways.

This is my explanation; it is quite abstract. I understand that it is quite confusing. But it is directly for immersing into the possibilities of this visualization, the technique of concentration on different figures, on different meanings of one state or another, of one influence or another of each figure.

And in terms of the triangle, everything begins directly from shifting the line, forming an angle.

And the larger this angle is, conditionally, the farther the two lines are from one another.

That is, the axis along which the movement was chosen, and the movement itself: as within this axis, on the boundary of the axis, or outside the axis.

The farther this movement is, the closer it is, from the originally chosen axis, to some other.

And, accordingly, development does not proceed in a purely spiritual, or social, or physical quality, and, accordingly, meditation does not go purely into this sphere; a mixture appears. And this is your task: to determine how you feel it, how you perceive it, how movement in meditation between the axes influences you.

Here it is important to have a certain overlaying projection of your life—that is, to determine what social development is for you, what physical is for you, what spiritual is for you; how you connect them, and what you get in this.

The figures of the universal matrix, multidimensional meditation, is a tool that helps you do this. And remember that the point itself in infinity is very complex; it is abstract, and we do not simply concentrate on it, but comprehend this idea. We do the same with the line; we do the same now as we begin to do with the triangle. And its first appearance is yet another point that forms an angle between the lines.
