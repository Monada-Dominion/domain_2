# 2.2.11 Multidimensional Meditation – Reflection

## The Triangle (Angle of Displacement) — Reflection

Use these prompts after practicing the triangle as a displaced line (2.2.9–2.2.10) to analyze your experience and calibrate the next session.

---

### Immediate Practice Notes

- Session focus: Did you spend more time on point → line, or on the angle itself?
- Base axis: Which axis did you choose first (up/down, left/right, toward/away)?
- Displacement: Toward which axis did you rotate the movement?
- Stability: Could you keep one point “fixed” while the other shifted?

---

### The Third Point (Factor)

- How did the “third point” appear in experience: as a decision, as an external force, as a feeling of tilt, as an emotional tone?
- Did the factor feel like something you control (choice), or something that controls you (habit, fear, desire, environment)?
- When the factor changed, did the probability/quality of realization change immediately, or slowly?

---

### Quality of Movement Between Axes

- Inside / boundary / outside: Could you feel the difference between movement inside an axis, on its boundary, and outside it?
- Mixing: Which mixture was most vivid?
	- spiritual ↔ social
	- social ↔ physical
	- spiritual ↔ physical
- Angle sensitivity: How small a displacement was enough to change the quality of the state?

---

### Personal Projection

- What do “physical,” “social,” and “spiritual” mean *for you* today (not in general)?
- Did the practice reveal an imbalance (for example, a constant drift toward one axis)?
- Did you discover a new interpretation of your own development through the angle metaphor?

---

### Integration (Daily Life)

- In a real situation today, where was your movement (axis), and what was the “factor” (third point) that changed its quality?
- Can you notice moments where your intention is “up,” but the motion becomes “diagonal”? What triggered that displacement?

---

### Troubleshooting and Next Step

- What was the main difficulty: abstraction, distraction, emotional resistance, or unstable visualization?
- One adjustment for the next session:
	- shorter session / longer session
	- stronger body anchor
	- smaller angle steps
	- clearer definition of axes
